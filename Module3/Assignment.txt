1. 802.11 PHY layer standards and comparison

The IEEE 802.11 PHY standards define how wireless signals are transmitted over the air. Early standards like 802.11 (1997) used FHSS and DSSS with very low data rates (1–2 Mbps). 
802.11b improved DSSS to reach up to 11 Mbps in the 2.4 GHz band. 802.11a introduced OFDM in the 5 GHz band, enabling up to 54 Mbps. 802.11g brought OFDM to 2.4 GHz with similar speeds.
802.11n introduced MIMO and channel bonding (20/40 MHz), increasing throughput up to 600 Mbps. 802.11ac operates in 5 GHz with wider channels (up to 160 MHz) and MU-MIMO, achieving multi-Gbps speeds.
802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) uses OFDMA, better efficiency, and operates in both 2.4 and 5 GHz (and 6 GHz in Wi-Fi 6E), improving performance in dense environments.

2. DSSS and FHSS

DSSS (Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum) spreads data by multiplying it with a pseudo-random sequence, increasing bandwidth and resistance to interference.
FHSS (Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum) transmits data by rapidly switching frequencies in a predefined pattern, reducing interference and improving security. 
DSSS provides higher data rates, while FHSS is more robust against narrowband interference.

3. Modulation schemes in PHY layer

Modulation converts digital data into radio signals. BPSK represents 1 bit per symbol, QPSK represents 2 bits, 16-QAM represents 4 bits, 64-QAM represents 6 bits, and 256-QAM represents 8 bits.
Higher-order modulation increases data rate but requires better signal quality (higher SNR). Lower modulation schemes are more robust but slower.

4. Significance of OFDM in WLAN

OFDM divides a wide channel into multiple orthogonal subcarriers, transmitting data in parallel. This reduces interference, improves spectral efficiency, and handles multipath fading effectively. 
It increases reliability and data rates in wireless environments.

5. Frequency bands and channels in Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi operates mainly in 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz bands.
2.4 GHz has fewer channels and more interference but better range.
5 GHz has more channels, less interference, and higher data rates but shorter range.
6 GHz provides even more spectrum, supporting high throughput and low latency. Channels are divided into fixed bandwidths like 20, 40, 80, and 160 MHz.

6. Guard Interval in WLAN

Guard Interval (GI) is a small time gap between OFDM symbols to prevent inter-symbol interference caused by multipath.
A shorter GI increases efficiency by reducing overhead, but it requires better channel conditions to avoid errors.

7. Structure of 802.11 PHY frame

The PHY frame consists of Preamble, Header, and Payload. The preamble is used for synchronization and channel estimation.
The header carries transmission parameters like rate and length. The payload contains the actual data (MAC frame).

8. OFDM vs OFDMA

OFDM assigns all subcarriers to a single user at a time. OFDMA divides subcarriers among multiple users simultaneously.
OFDMA improves efficiency and reduces latency in dense networks.

9. MIMO vs MU-MIMO

MIMO uses multiple antennas to improve data rate and reliability for a single user. MU-MIMO allows multiple users to receive data simultaneously using spatial streams, increasing overall network capacity.

10. PPDU, PLCP, PMD

PPDU (PLCP Protocol Data Unit) is the complete PHY frame transmitted over the air.
PLCP (Physical Layer Convergence Protocol) prepares MAC data for transmission and adds headers.
PMD (Physical Medium Dependent) handles actual signal transmission, modulation, and radio characteristics.

11. Types of PPDU

Different Wi-Fi generations define different PPDU formats such as Legacy PPDU, HT PPDU (802.11n), VHT PPDU (802.11ac), and HE PPDU (802.11ax). 
Each adds new fields to support features like MIMO, wider channels, and OFDMA while maintaining backward compatibility.

12. Data rate calculation

Data rate depends on bandwidth, modulation, coding rate, number of spatial streams, and guard interval. Increasing channel width, using higher modulation, and adding spatial streams increases data rate.
Lower guard intervals also improve throughput.
